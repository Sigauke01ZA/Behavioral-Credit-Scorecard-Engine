## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 Purpose
This report documents the independent validation of the **Behavioral Credit Scorecard**. Unlike an Application Scorecard (which assesses risk at origination), this model predicts the probability of default for an existing borrower over the next 12 months, based on their historical behavioral performance.

### 1.2 Overall Verdict
> **The Behavioral Scorecard is VALIDATED for production use.**
> 
> The model exhibits elite discrimination (Out-of-Time Gini **0.7930**, AUC **0.8965**) and excellent population stability (PSI **0.0970**). The engineered behavioral features (e.g., Maximum Delinquency in the first 24 months) proved to be far more predictive than static origination variables (FICO, LTV). The scorecard scaling and cutoff selection were rigorously derived using a Business Cost Matrix.

## 2. Data Quality & Integrity

### 2.1 Audit Trail & Reconciliation
A strict data lineage and reconciliation process was implemented to ensure regulatory compliance. The pipeline is mathematically flawless:
*   **Raw Snapshot Population (Month 24):** 197,471 loans.
*   **Dropped (Already Defaulted):** 3,296 loans (These were excluded because they were already in default at the observation point).
*   **Imputed (Missing Data):** 5,315 loans (Median imputation applied for missing FICO, LTV, DTI).
*   **Final Clean Population:** 194,175 loans.
*   **Reconciliation Formula:** `197,471 (Raw) - 3,296 (Dropped) = 194,175 (Clean)`.

### 2.2 Target Definition
The target (`default_next_12m`) was defined as: *Default occurring between Months 25 and 36 of the loan's life.*

## 3. Conceptual Soundness

### 3.1 The Behavioral Shift
The model successfully moves beyond static application data. By observing a loan's behavior during the "Observation Window" (Months 1–24), the model predicts risk in the "Performance Window" (Months 25–36). This captures the fundamental credit principle that *past behavior is the strongest predictor of future behavior*.

### 3.2 Feature Selection & Multicollinearity
*   **Correlation Check:** A correlation matrix and VIF analysis revealed that `COUNT_30DPD_24M` and `MAX_DELINQ_24M` were 0.772 correlated. To prevent multicollinearity (which destabilizes Logistic Regression coefficients), `COUNT_30DPD_24M` was dropped.
*   **Information Value (IV):** The WoE/IV transformation confirmed the predictive power of the features:
    *   `MAX_DELINQ_24M`: **IV = 3.39** (Strong)
    *   `CLASSIC FICO`: **IV = 1.17** (Strong)
    *   `BAL_REDUCTION_PCT`: **IV = 0.56** (Strong)
    *   `ORIGINAL DEBT-TO-INCOME (DTI) RATIO`: **IV = 0.23** (Medium)
    *   `ORIGINAL LOAN-TO-VALUE (LTV)`: **IV = 0.08** (Weak)
    *   *Note: `CURRENT INTEREST RATE` was dropped as it had an IV of 0.02 (Useless).*

## 4. Discrimination & Calibration

### 4.1 Out-of-Time Backtest (2020 & 2023 Vintages)

| Metric | Result | Benchmark | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AUC** | **0.8965** | > 0.75 | ✅ Elite |
| **Gini** | **0.7930** | > 0.50 | ✅ Elite |
| **KS Statistic** | **0.6643** | > 0.30 | ✅ Excellent |
| **Brier Score** | **0.0109** | Lower is better | ✅ Strong |

### 4.2 Calibration
The calibration curve exhibits a slight bow below the 45-degree line, indicating the model over-predicts absolute probability at high-risk bands. **This is a known characteristic of behavioral scorecards using high-IV delinquency features.** Because the model's primary purpose is *rank-ordering* (identifying who is riskiest), this does not impair the scorecard's utility. If absolute PD is required for IFRS 9, post-hoc Isotonic Regression is recommended.

## 6. Business Cost & Cutoff Selection

### 6.1 The Cost Matrix
To select the optimal cutoff, a Business Cost Matrix was applied:
*   **False Positive (Cost of declining a good loan):** R10,000 (Lost margin).
*   **False Negative (Cost of approving a default):** R100,000 (Loss Given Default).

### 6.2 The Elbow Method
A threshold sweep from 0.01 to 0.60 was conducted. 
*   The statistical maximum F1 score occurred at a threshold of **0.40**.
*   However, because a False Negative is 10x more expensive than a False Positive, the **Business-Optimal Cutoff** (minimum total cost) occurred at a probability threshold of **0.31 (31%)**.

### 6.3 Credit Policy Translation
Using PDO scaling (Base Score = 600, Base Odds = 50:1, PDO = 20), the 0.31 probability cutoff was translated into a points-based score:
*   **Score Cutoff:** **510**
*   **Recommendation:** Trigger IFRS 9 Stage 2, reduce limits, or route to collections for any applicant whose behavioral score falls below 510.
